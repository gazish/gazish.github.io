Online retail sales: SQL, Python and a KPI dashboard

Two years of invoice lines from a UK online giftware retailer, taken from a raw export to cleaned SQL tables, defined KPIs, customer cohorts and segments. Every number on this page comes from the code in the repository.

  • Go to the dashboard
  • Code and SQL on GitHub
Data
UCI Online Retail II, CC BY 4.0. 1,067,371 invoice lines, Dec 2009 to Dec 2011
Tools
SQL (DuckDB), Python (pandas), JavaScript for the dashboard
Output
9 SQL files, a checked pipeline, CSV outputs and this dashboard
Built
November 2026, as a portfolio project

Key findings

Net sales were flat (+1.9%) between the two 12-month periods, and sales to identified customers fell 1.3%. The growth that did happen came from international markets and from sales with no customer ID.

  • UK flat, international up. UK net sales changed by -0.1%; international net sales grew 14.1% and reached 15.5% of the total.
  • Fewer, larger orders. Orders fell 4.0% while average order value rose 6.8%, from £476 to £508.
  • A measurement gap is growing. Sales without a customer ID rose from 11.3% to 14.6% of gross sales, so customer KPIs cover less of the business than a year earlier.
  • Sales are concentrated. The top 10% of customers brought 60.0% of net sales. 371 "At risk" customers (median four orders, 116 days since the last one) are a ready-made reactivation list worth £555k a year.

P1 = Dec 2009 to Nov 2010, P2 = Dec 2010 to Nov 2011. December 2011 has only nine days of data and is left out.

    Problem

    The question I set myself: if I joined this retailer as an analyst, what would management need to see each month, and can the raw export support it? The file is a typical operational export. It has duplicate lines, cancellations mixed in with sales, postage and bank fees recorded as products, accounting adjustments, and no customer ID on almost a quarter of the lines.

    Approach

    1. Load and profileLoaded the CSV into DuckDB with explicit types and profiled every column: date range, invoice prefixes, stock codes that are not products, missing IDs.
    2. Clean with explicit rulesRemoved exact duplicates and gave every line a type (sale, cancellation, fee, write-off, adjustment). Nothing is silently dropped: the table below accounts for every raw row.
    3. Define the KPIs before computing themNet sales, orders, average order value, active customers, repeat rate, retention, cancellation rate and the share of sales without a customer ID, each with a written definition.
    4. Build the metrics in SQLMonthly and 12-month KPIs by market with grouping sets, acquisition cohorts, RFM scores with window functions, and country and product summaries.
    5. Check the numbersThe Python script reconciles totals across tables and markets, then recomputes the headline KPIs in pandas, independently of the SQL, and stops if anything disagrees.
    6. PresentExported compact aggregates (38 KB of JSON) for this dashboard, with a table view for every chart.
    Example query: RFM scores with window functions 
    scored AS (
    SELECT
        *,
        -- quintile scores; equal values always get the same score
        ceil(5 * cume_dist() OVER (ORDER BY recency_days DESC))::INT  AS r_score,  -- most recent = 5
        CASE
            WHEN frequency = 1 THEN 1
            WHEN frequency = 2 THEN 2
            WHEN frequency BETWEEN 3 AND 4 THEN 3
            WHEN frequency BETWEEN 5 AND 9 THEN 4
            ELSE 5
        END                                                           AS f_score,  -- fixed bins: 36% have one order
        ceil(5 * cume_dist() OVER (ORDER BY monetary))::INT          AS m_score
    FROM base
)

    Most duplicates (22,844 rows) come from 1 to 9 December 2010, which appears in both yearly sheets of the source workbook. The rest (11,491 rows, 0.3% of sales value) are identical lines on the same invoice within the same minute, which I also treat as duplicates.

    Tools

    • SQL in DuckDB: CTEs, grouping sets, window functions (cume_dist), arg_min, filtered aggregates.
    • Python with pandas: orchestration, reconciliation checks, an independent recomputation of the headline KPIs, the revenue bridge and the concentration curve.
    • JavaScript and SVG: the dashboard on this page, with no chart library.

    Result

    A pipeline that turns the raw file into a small set of defined, reconciled KPIs in a few seconds, and a dashboard that answers three questions: is the business growing, where is the change coming from, and which customers matter most. The full list of findings and the KPI definitions are in the project README.

    What I would do next

    • Find out why almost a quarter of lines have no customer ID, and whether those sales can be matched to accounts. Until then, customer KPIs understate the business.
    • Add cost data so the dashboard can show gross margin, not only sales.
    • Map stock codes to product categories and add category KPIs.
    • Test a reactivation offer on the "At risk" segment against a holdout group, and measure the difference in orders.
    • Schedule the pipeline and turn the checks into automated data tests.
