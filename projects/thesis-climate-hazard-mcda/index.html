Multi-Criteria Threat Evaluation for Local Climate Hazards

A reusable decision-support template that helps municipalities decide which local climate hazards to prioritise, and shows how sure they can be of that order. Co-written with Vladimir Blaskov.

  • Try a related interactive tool
Degree
MSc in Data Science, Statistics and Decision Analysis, Stockholm University
Co-author
Vladimir Blaskov
Cases
Tampere (Finland), Tartu (Estonia), Malmö (Sweden)

Problem

A municipality faces several local climate hazards at once and cannot address all of them at the same time. Deciding which to prioritise means comparing very different threats on several criteria, and the people involved do not weigh those criteria the same way. Many of the inputs are also uncertain. A one-off ranking that hides these choices is difficult to defend or to repeat elsewhere.

Approach

We built a multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) template that a municipality can fill in with its own hazards and stakeholders. It has five parts:

  1. Weighted criteriaTo judge every hazard on the same terms: a set of criteria, each with a weight.
  2. Rubric-based scoringFor threats that are hard to compare: each hazard is scored against written rubric levels for each criterion.
  3. Stakeholder priorities from a surveyFor people who weigh the criteria differently: stakeholder priorities elicited with a survey.
  4. Uncertainty ratingsFor uncertain inputs: a rating of how uncertain they are.
  5. Sensitivity analysisTo show how much the ranking depends on those inputs: best- and worst-case rank intervals for each hazard.

We demonstrated the template on municipality cases in the Baltic Sea Region: Tampere, Tartu and Malmö.

Methods

MCDA, rubric scoring, survey elicitation, uncertainty ratings, sensitivity analysis.

Result

A reusable procedure: a municipality's hazards, its stakeholders' priorities and its own scores go in, and a ranked list with a rank interval for each hazard comes out.

The cases from Tampere, Tartu and Malmö, and their results, are in the full thesis on DiVA.

What I would do next

  • Apply the template in more municipalities and compare how stakeholder priorities differ between them.
  • Repeat the stakeholder survey over time to see whether priorities change.
  • Check whether the ranking holds under other aggregation methods, as a further robustness test.
  • Turn the template into a simple web tool that a municipality can use with less analyst support. The decision analysis tool on this site shares some general building blocks (weights, sensitivity analysis, rank intervals), but not the template's rubric scoring or survey.
